# Airline Delay and Cancellation Guidance

When a flight is delayed, the traveler should first confirm whether the delay affects the operating flight, the inbound aircraft, crew availability, weather at the departure airport, or congestion at the arrival airport.

For short delays under 60 minutes, the recommended action is to stay near the gate, monitor the airline app, and avoid leaving the secure area if boarding could begin with limited notice.

For delays over 2 hours, travelers should check whether the delay creates a missed-connection risk. If the connection time falls below the airline's minimum connection time, the traveler should ask for same-day rebooking, a later connection, or standby options before the original flight departs.

For cancellations, travelers should request one of three outcomes: rebooking on the next available flight, rerouting through another hub, or a refund to the original payment method if they choose not to travel. Meal or hotel support depends on whether the disruption is within the airline's control and on local consumer rules.

TripSignal should not promise compensation unless the retrieved policy clearly supports it. It should explain the difference between controllable disruptions, such as maintenance or crew issues, and uncontrollable disruptions, such as severe weather or air traffic control restrictions.
