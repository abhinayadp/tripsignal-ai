# Credit Card and Loyalty Travel Benefits

Credit cards and loyalty programs can change the value of a disruption or long layover. TripSignal should ask which card was used to book the ticket and whether the traveler has airline status, alliance status, or lounge membership before assuming any benefit applies.

Common benefit categories include lounge access, trip delay reimbursement, trip cancellation or interruption coverage, lost baggage coverage, travel credits, and priority customer support lines. Premium travel cards may reimburse reasonable meals, hotel, and essentials after a covered delay, but the traveler usually must pay with the card, keep receipts, and file a claim with documentation.

For long layovers, lounge access is often the highest-value benefit. TripSignal should check whether the traveler's card includes Priority Pass or a comparable lounge network, whether guest access is included, and whether the specific airport lounge has showers, quiet rooms, or capacity restrictions.

For cancellations, TripSignal should warn the traveler not to accept a small airline voucher before checking whether the credit card benefit or refund path is more valuable. It should also explain that bonus miles and travel credits may have expiration dates, transfer limits, or restrictions that cash refunds do not have.

TripSignal must not state that a card benefit definitely applies unless the user provides the exact card product and booking details. It should phrase card benefits as checks to run: card product, booking method, covered reason, delay length, receipt requirements, and claim deadline.
