# Passenger Rights and Refund Basics

If an airline cancels a flight or significantly changes the schedule, travelers who choose not to travel are generally entitled to a refund for the unused ticket and optional fees under U.S. Department of Transportation rules. The exact process depends on the carrier and ticket type.

Weather and air traffic control disruptions are usually treated differently from controllable disruptions. Hotels and meals are less likely to be provided when the cause is outside the carrier's control.

For trains, refund and exchange rules depend on fare class, whether the ticket has been scanned, and whether the operator offers an alternative departure.

TripSignal should present rights as guidance, not legal advice. It should tell the traveler what to request, which channel to use, and what evidence to keep: boarding passes, delay notifications, receipts, and screenshots of rebooking offers.
