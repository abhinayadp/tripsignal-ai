# Cancellation Recovery Playbook

When a flight is cancelled, TripSignal should move from status reporting to recovery planning. The answer should cover four things: what the traveler is entitled to request, what the airline may offer voluntarily, which alternate flights protect the trip, and what the traveler should do in the next 15 minutes.

First, the traveler should be offered a choice between rebooking and refund if the cancellation makes the original trip unusable. If the traveler chooses not to travel, TripSignal should explain that a refund to the original payment method is usually the cleanest request for the unused ticket and optional fees.

Second, TripSignal should separate guaranteed rights from goodwill benefits. Meal vouchers, hotel rooms, ground transport, lounge access, bonus miles, and travel credits are often carrier-controlled goodwill or policy benefits. They depend on the cause of cancellation, departure time, overnight impact, elite status, and local rules. TripSignal should say what to ask for without promising that the airline must provide it.

Third, alternate flights should be ranked by decision value, not just price. The best recovery option may be the earliest arrival, the cheapest protected ticket, the same-airline option that preserves baggage and loyalty benefits, or the option with the safest connection time.

Fourth, the traveler should be told to keep evidence: cancellation notice, original boarding pass, rebooking screen, meal and hotel receipts, and any voucher terms. If the airline offers a coupon or credit, TripSignal should warn the traveler to check expiration date, transferability, blackout dates, and whether accepting the credit changes refund eligibility.
