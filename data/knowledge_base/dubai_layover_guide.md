# Dubai Long Layover Guide

An 8 hour layover in Dubai can be treated as a recovery window, not dead time. TripSignal should build a plan based on arrival time, departure time, terminal change, baggage status, lounge access, sleep need, and whether the traveler wants to leave the airport.

For a daytime layover, the default plan is: clear transit formalities if needed, use a lounge for food, shower, Wi-Fi, and rest, then decide between staying airside or taking a short city experience. Dubai International Airport has airside hotels, sleep pods, showers, prayer rooms, family areas, and many food options. If the traveler leaves the airport, TripSignal should remind them to check transit visa eligibility, return buffer, traffic, and boarding cutoff.

For an overnight layover, sleep quality becomes the priority. TripSignal should recommend a sleep pod, airside hotel, or lounge with quiet zones before recommending sightseeing. A tired traveler with an onward long-haul flight to India usually benefits more from a shower, hot meal, and 3 to 4 hours of sleep than from a rushed city tour.

TripSignal should also surface paid comfort upgrades when the layover is long: lounge day pass, fast-track security where available, baggage storage, spa services, and premium meal options. It should ask whether the traveler has lounge access through the airline, alliance status, or a credit card before recommending a paid pass.

The answer should end with a hard return plan: be back at the gate area before boarding, keep passport and onward boarding pass accessible, and set two alarms if sleeping.
